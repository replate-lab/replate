What this model is, and is not

It is a lumped-parameter (0-D) transient model: one coupled energy balance and one mass-transfer relation, integrated forward in time. This is the standard tool for sizing a dryer and for finding where an operating optimum sits.

It is not CFD and it is not FEA. There is no spatial resolution — the load is treated as one well-mixed lump at a single temperature. It cannot tell you about dead zones, channelling, or hot spots on the vessel wall.

Every number it produces is a prediction to be tested, not a result. The point of building the machine is to find out where this model is wrong.

Assumptions

  • Well-mixed load at uniform temperature — reasonable with an agitator running, less so between mixing intervals.
  • Exhaust approaches saturation to a factor φ = 0.60, falling linearly once moisture content drops below Xc = 0.6 kg/kg dry. This is what produces the falling-rate period.
  • Wall loss from conduction through insulation plus 10 W/m²K outside convection, multiplied by 2.5 for thermal bridging through the lid, shaft and supports. That factor is an estimate and Phase 2 measures it.
  • Constant properties — cp of solids 1.8 kJ/kg·K, water 4.18. Latent heat varies with temperature as hfg = 2501 − 2.36·T kJ/kg.
  • Altitude matters. At 1300 m the ambient pressure is about 87 kPa, which raises saturation humidity and helps drying. Sea-level figures understate it.
  • Parasitic loads included: blower scaled with airflow, agitator at its 20 s / 8 min duty.
